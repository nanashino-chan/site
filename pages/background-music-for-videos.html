Three decisions before publishing

  1. What should the music do for this scene?
  2. Where and how will the video be published?
  3. Does the music permission cover that exact use?

Quick answer: how do you choose background music for a video?

Start with the format, pace and role of speech. Audition instrumental tracks in the finished edit at a realistic playback level. Before publishing, check the terms for your platform, monetization, business or client use, and paid promotion. Listening to or crediting a track does not by itself grant video use rights.

Match the music to the video

The best background track is the one that helps viewers follow the content. These are starting points for auditioning, not fixed rules for a genre or a platform.

Make the music work in the edit

The right track can still feel wrong when it competes with dialogue or ends awkwardly. A quick check in context is more useful than judging the music on its own.

Cut with the scene

Find a natural musical phrase for the opening, a transition or the ending. Check that your final fade does not interrupt a beat or melody unexpectedly.

Prioritize speech

Lower the music under narration and listen for frequencies that mask words. Test quiet speakers or a phone, not only studio headphones.

Watch short-form pacing

Look at the first few seconds with sound on and off. The footage, captions and message should still make sense when the viewer cannot hear the music.

Keep a project record

Save the track title, recording source, license terms and receipt with the final export. This helps with client handoff or a platform question later.

Choose permission for the way you publish

The video format alone does not decide the rights. A personal post, monetized YouTube upload, company video, client deliverable and paid ad can need different permissions. Always check the actual terms of the track you select.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use any song if I credit the artist?

Credit alone is not permission. Check the specific track’s license for your video, platform and purpose. Some libraries also require attribution as a condition of their terms.

Can I use Nanashino-chan music in monetized YouTube videos?

Eligible monetized creator videos can be covered by the Commercial Creator License when the track and use fit its terms. Keep the certificate and receipt. Paid ads and brand campaigns require Sync License or custom terms.

What if I post the same edit on several platforms?

Check that the agreement covers every destination and the account publishing it. A license or in-app sound available on one service may not cover use on another.

Is “royalty-free” the same as free of copyright?

No. It is a description of a licensing model, not proof that a recording has no rights holder or is unrestricted. Read the permitted uses, limitations and documentation.

Can a licensed track still receive a Content ID claim?

Yes. Automated matching and rights-holder permission are separate processes. If a claim affects approved licensed use, retain the claim details and license proof, then follow the platform process and contact Nanashino-chan for support.
