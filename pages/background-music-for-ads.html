What license does background music for ads need?

For a paid video ad using an existing recording, check permission to synchronize the composition and master recording with your visuals, and make sure the agreement covers the intended advertising use. Nanashino-chan directs paid ads and brand campaigns to a Sync License or a custom agreement. The standard Commercial Creator License is for eligible non-ad content; it does not cover paid ads.

Choose a soundtrack that serves the ad

Good background music supports the story, pacing and product. Test the edit with dialogue on, on a phone speaker and at the volume your audience will actually hear.

01 · Message

Protect the voiceover

Leave space for speech, product sounds and the call to action. A restrained instrumental can help when the spoken message carries the sale.

02 · Mood

Match the product

Warm grooves can suit lifestyle and food; lighter textures can support software demos; atmospheric music can add polish to fashion or design visuals.

03 · Edit

Plan the ending

Check the opening seconds, any transition and the final logo or offer. Cut or fade the music deliberately rather than letting the track stop mid-phrase.

Music availability is different from ad permission

A track being free to hear, downloadable, or visible in an app does not by itself establish permission for a paid campaign. Conversely, a free track can be appropriate when its actual terms explicitly cover the planned advertising use.

From first cut to campaign launch

Map the media plan

List the advertiser or client, intended platforms, territories, dates, paid placements, video versions and any creator account used for promotion.

Audition the music

Try the track in the actual edit. Check the voiceover, first three seconds, product reveal, final call to action and mobile playback.

Confirm the license

Match the selected recording and project to the Sync License terms. Ask in advance about additional platforms, whitelisting, extended use or custom media.

Archive proof and test

Save the license certificate, purchase record, track details and campaign plan. Follow each platform's ad review process and retain claim details if an issue appears.

Details worth checking before purchase

  • Track title and the exact recording
  • Licensee, brand, agency and client roles
  • Platforms and paid placements
  • Campaign dates and continued availability afterward
  • Territories and any offline media
  • Ad variations, edits and creator whitelisting
  • Required add-ons and license documents
  • Platform review time before the launch date

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a song from Spotify or Apple Music in a video ad?

A listening or download subscription does not itself license synchronization with an ad. Obtain the permissions required for the composition and the recording, and check the advertising scope.

Can I boost a Reel that already uses music?

Boosting changes the distribution to paid advertising. Check whether the existing music permission covers that use before promoting the post. For Nanashino-chan music, review the Sync License or request custom terms.

Is free music always unsuitable for ads?

No. Some free or stock music licenses allow specific commercial uses. Read the actual terms for paid advertising, the planned platforms, duration and any restrictions before relying on them.

Does a Sync License prevent every Content ID claim or ad rejection?

No. A license documents permitted use within its terms. Automated claims and platform ad reviews can still occur. Keep the certificate and receipt; contact Nanashino-chan for support with a claim involving approved licensed use.

Is one license enough for several platforms or a client campaign?

Only if those uses are within the agreed terms. Confirm platforms, client and licensee, ad account arrangements, duration and any multi-platform or whitelisting add-on before launch.
