How to Reduce YouTube Content ID Claims for Background Music

By Nanashino-chan, an independent instrumental music project in Tokyo

Before you publish a YouTube video, check whether your permission covers the exact recording, your channel, and the way you plan to use it. Save the license and review YouTube Studio’s copyright checks. A licensed track can still trigger a Content ID match, so keep the paperwork handy.

Content ID claim or copyright strike?

A Content ID claim is a match to material registered in YouTube’s system. Depending on the rights holder’s policy, the video may be tracked, monetized, or blocked, sometimes only in certain territories. A copyright strike follows a valid copyright removal request. Check the restriction shown in YouTube Studio before deciding how to respond.

Before uploading: check the track, the recording, and the license

  1. Identify the recording. Write down the track title, artist, and version. If available, save the ISRC to help identify the recording. The ISRC identifies a recording; your license documents permission.
  2. Check both sets of rights. A video can involve rights in the composition and in the recording. If you license a recorded track from a library or directly from an artist, check what their permission covers for both.
  3. Match the permission to the project. Check the upload channel, monetization, client use, paid promotion, and any territory or duration limits that apply. Ask whether the provider requires channel registration or offers a way to release claims.
  4. Save proof and review the upload. Keep the license, order reference, and any written permissions with the project files. Upload privately and review the copyright check before making the video public. A claim may still appear later.

For example, if you edit a product demo for a client, check whether the license covers the client’s channel and whether paid distribution is included. If the editor added music through an in-app library, check the terms before exporting that audio to YouTube or handing off a version for an ad.

What to do if YouTube Studio shows a claim

  1. Open the claim details. In YouTube Studio, find the video under Content and open the copyright restriction details. Check the claimant, the matched timestamps, and how the claim affects the video.
  2. Compare the match with your records. If you licensed the recording, check the exact version and the scope of the license. Contact the provider with the video URL, track title, claimant name, and license reference if it offers claim support.
  3. Choose the response that fits. If you have a valid basis, such as permission covering the matched use, you can dispute the claim in Studio. If you lack permission, consider trimming the segment, replacing the song, or erasing it using the options available in Studio. Crediting the artist, buying a copy, or leaving monetization off does not by itself justify a dispute.
